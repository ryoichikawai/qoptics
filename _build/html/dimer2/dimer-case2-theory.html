13.1. Dimer II: Theory#

13.1.1. Equations of motion#

The diagonal elements do not couple to the off-diagonal elements, and their equations of motion are

\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}\rho_{gg} &= - 2 \Gamma_{11} N_{T} \rho_{gg} + \Gamma_{s}(N_{T}+1) \rho_{ss} + \Gamma^{-} (N_{T}+1) \rho_{aa}\\ \frac{d}{dt}\rho_{aa} &= - \Gamma_{a}(2 N_{T}+1) \rho_{aa} + \Gamma_{a} N_{T} \rho_{gg} + \Gamma^{-} (N_{T}+1) \rho_{ee} \\ \frac{d}{dt}\rho_{ss} &= - \Gamma_{s}(2 N_{T}+1) \rho_{ss} + \Gamma_{s} N_{T} \rho_{gg} + \Gamma^{+} (N_{T}+1) \rho_{ee} \\ \frac{d}{dt}\rho_{ee} &= - 2 \Gamma_{11} (N_{T}+1) \rho_{ee} + \Gamma_{a} N_{T} \rho_{aa} + \Gamma^{+} N_{T} \rho_{ss} \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

where \(\Gamma_{s} = \Gamma_{11}+\Gamma_{12}\) and \(\Gamma_{a} = \Gamma_{11}-\Gamma_{12}\). One equation can be eliminated using the normalization condition \(\rho_{gg}+\rho_{aa}+\rho_{ss}+\rho_{ee}=1\).

\[\begin{split} \frac{d}{dt} \begin{pmatrix} \rho_{gg} \\ \rho_{aa} \\ \rho_{ss} \\ \rho_{ee} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} - 2 \Gamma_{11} N_{T} & \Gamma_{a} (N_{T}+1) & \Gamma_{s}(N_{T}+1) & 0 \\ \Gamma_{s} N_{T} & - \Gamma_{a}(2 N_{T}+1) & 0 & \Gamma_{a} (N_{T}+1) \\ \Gamma^{+} N_{T} & 0 & - \Gamma_{s}(2 N_{T}+1) & \Gamma_{s} (N_{T}+1) \\ 0 & \Gamma_{a} N_{T} & \Gamma_{s} N_{T} & - 2 \Gamma_{11} (N_{T}+1) \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \rho_{gg} \\ \rho_{aa} \\ \rho_{ss} \\ \rho_{ee} \end{pmatrix} \end{split}\]

The steady state solution is

(13.1)#\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} \rho_{gg}^{ss} &= \frac{(1+N_{T})^2}{(1+2 N_{T})^2} = \frac{1}{(1+e^{-\omega_{0}/T})^2}\\ \rho_{ss}^{ss} &= \frac{N_{T} (N_{T}+1)}{(1+2 N_{T})^2} = \frac{e^{-\omega_{0}/T}}{(1+e^{-\omega_{0}/T})^2} \\ \rho_{aa}^{ss} &= \frac{N_{T} (N_{T}+1)}{(1+2 N_{T})^2} = \frac{e^{-\omega_{0}/T}}{(1+e^{-\omega_{0}/T})^2} \\ \rho_{ee}^{ss} &= \frac{N_{T}^2}{(1+2 N_{T})^2} = \frac{e^{-2\omega_{0}/T}}{(1+e^{-\omega_{0}/T})^2} \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

which agrees with the Boltzmann distribution. Notice that neither \(\Gamma_{11}\) nor \(\Gamma_{12}\) appears in the steady state because the thermal equilibrium only depends on the energy eigenvalues.

Thanks to the Dicke basis, the equations of motion for the off-diagonal elements are rather simple. First, two elements are self-contained as

\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt} \rho_{as} &= - \Gamma_{11} (2 N_{T}+1) \rho_{as} \\ \frac{d}{dt} \rho_{ge} &= \left[ i 2\omega_{0} - \Gamma_{11}(2 N_{T}+1) \right] \rho_{ge} \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

which can be solved right away

\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} \rho_{as}(t) &= e^{-\Gamma_{11} (2 N_{T}+1) t} \rho_{as}(0) \\ \rho_{ge}(t) &= e^{i 2\omega_{0} t} e^{-\Gamma_{11} (2 N_{T}+1) t} \rho_{ge}(0) \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

Both decay quickly at the rate of \(\Gamma_{11} (2 N_{T}+1)\).

The equations of motion for the off-diagonal elements inside the channel 1 are a pair of ODEs:

\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt} \rho_{gs} &= \left[ i \omega_{0} - \Gamma_{11} N_{T} - \Gamma_{s} \left(N_{T}+\frac{1}{2}\right) \right]\rho_{gs} + \Gamma_{s}(N_{T}+1) \rho_{se}\\ \frac{d}{dt} \rho_{se} &= \left[ i \omega_{0} - \Gamma_{11} (N_{T}+1) - \Gamma_{s} \left(N_{T}+\frac{1}{2}\right) \right] \rho_{se} + \Gamma_{s}N_{T} \rho_{gs} \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

It is easy to find that \(\rho_{gs}\) and \(\rho_{se}\) vanish as \(t \rightarrow \infty\).

Similarly for channel 2, we have a coupled ODE:

\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt} \rho_{ga} &= \left[ i \omega_{0} - \Gamma_{11} N_{T} - \Gamma_{a} \left(N_{T}+\frac{1}{2}\right) \right]\rho_{ga} + \Gamma_{a}(N_{T}+1) \rho_{ae}\\ \frac{d}{dt} \rho_{ae} &= \left[ i \omega_{0} - \Gamma_{11} (N_{T}+1) - \Gamma_{a} \left(N_{T}+\frac{1}{2}\right) \right] \rho_{ae} + (\Gamma_{a}N_{T} \rho_{ga} \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

and \(\rho_{ga}\) and \(\rho_{ae}\) vanish as \(t \rightarrow \infty\).

Since all off-diagonal elements vanish in the steady state, there is no coherence between the Dicke basis states.

13.1.2. Broken Ergodicity#

Recall that we are taking the limit \(\displaystyle\lim_{\Gamma_{12} \rightarrow \gamma_0}\). At the same time, we take another limit \(\displaystyle\lim_{t \rightarrow \infty}\) to get the steady state. These two limits do not commute, that is \(\displaystyle\lim_{\Gamma_{12} \rightarrow \gamma_0} \lim_{t \rightarrow \infty} \rho \ne \lim_{t \rightarrow \infty} \lim_{\Gamma_{12} \rightarrow \gamma_0} \rho\). The steady state (13.1) is obtained by \(\displaystyle\lim_{\Gamma_{12} \rightarrow \gamma_0} \lim_{t \rightarrow \infty} \rho\).
If we take the limit \(\displaystyle\lim_{\Gamma_{12} \rightarrow \gamma_0}\) first, the anti-symmetric state \(|a\rangle\) is completely isolated from the radiation field. Furthermore, it is an eigenstate of the Hamiltonian. There is no process that changes its population. Because of this isolation, \(\rho_{aa}^{ss} = \rho_{aa}(0)\) and the system does not reach the thermal state (13.1). The steady state depends on the initial condition! In thermodynamics, this issue is known as broken ergodicity.

When the ergodicity is broken, we need to remove \(|a\rangle\) from the Hilbert space. Then, the steady state should be

(13.2)#\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} \rho_{gg}^{ss} &= \frac{(1+N_{T})^2}{3 N_{T}^2 + 3 N_{T}+1} = \frac{e^{\omega_{0}/T}}{1 + e^{-\omega_{0}/T} + e^{+\omega_{0}/T}}\\ \rho_{ss}^{ss} &= \frac{N_{T} (N_{T}+1)}{3 N_{T}^2 + 3 N_{T}+1} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\omega_{0}/T} + e^{+\omega_{0}/T}} \\ \rho_{aa}^{ss} &= 0 \\ \rho_{ee}^{ss} &= \frac{N_{T}^2}{3 N_{T}^2 + 3 N_{T}+1} = \frac{e^{-\omega_{0}/T}}{1 + e^{-\omega_{0}/T} + e^{+\omega_{0}/T}} \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

We will see that the asymptotic limit \(\Gamma_{12} \rightarrow \gamma_0\) does not agree with \(\Gamma_{12}=\gamma_{0}\).

13.1.3. Intensity of radiation#

The intensity of radiation measured at \(\mathbf{R}\) is given by Eq. (7.15). For a dimer, it is

\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} I(\mathbf{R},t) &= \Gamma(\mathbf{R}) \left[\langle\sigma^{+}_{1}\sigma^{-}_{1}\rangle_{ss} + \langle\sigma^{+}_{2}\sigma^{-}_{2}\rangle_{ss} + e^{i \phi } \langle\sigma^{+}_{1}\sigma^{-}_{2}\rangle_{ss} + e^{- i \phi} \langle\sigma^{+}_{2}\sigma^{-}_{1}\rangle_{ss} \right] \\ & \approx \Gamma(\mathbf{R}) \left[\langle\sigma^{+}_{1}\sigma^{-}_{1}\rangle_{ss} + \langle\sigma^{+}_{2}\sigma^{-}_{2}\rangle_{ss} + \left(1+i \phi - \frac{\phi^2}{2}\right)\langle\sigma^{+}_{1}\sigma^{-}_{2}\rangle_{ss} + \left(1-i \phi - \frac{\phi^2}{2}\right)\langle\sigma^{+}_{2}\sigma^{-}_{1}\rangle_{ss} \right] \\ &= \Gamma(\mathbf{R}) \left[ \left(2-\frac{\phi^2}{2}\right) \langle \eta^{+}_{1}\eta^{-}_{1}\rangle_{ss} + \frac{\phi^2}{2} \langle \eta^{+}_{2}\eta^{-}_{2}\rangle_{ss} + i \phi \left(\langle \eta^{+}_{1}\eta^{-}_{2}\rangle_{ss} -\langle \eta^{+}_{2}\eta^{-}_{1}\rangle_{ss}\right) \right] \\ &= \Gamma(\mathbf{R}) \left[ \left(2-\frac{\phi^2}{2}\right) \langle \eta^{+}_{1}\eta^{-}_{1}\rangle_{ss} + \frac{\phi^2}{2} \langle \eta^{+}_{2}\eta^{-}_{2}\rangle_{ss} \right] \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

where \(\phi = k_{0}r_{12} \cos(\chi)\) with the angle \(\chi\) between \(\mathbf{R}\) and \(\mathbf{r}_{12}\). Since we assume \(r_{12} \ll \lambda_{0}\) (\(\phi \ll 1\)) we expand the phase factor up to \(\phi^2\). The cross correlations \(\langle \eta^{+}_{1} \eta^{-}_{2}\rangle_{ss}\) and \(\langle \eta^{+}_{2} \eta^{-}_{1}\rangle_{ss}\) vanish since \(\rho^{ss}_{as} = \rho^{ss}_{sa}=0\). Here \(\Gamma(\mathbf{R}) = \left(\frac{\omega_{0}^{2}d}{c^2}\right)^2 \left [ 1 - \left(\hat{\mathbf{d}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{R}}\right)^2\right]\).

Interestingly, when \(\phi=0\) (either \(r_{12}=0\) or \(\chi=\pi/2\)), \(\eta^{\pm}_{2}\) (a-channel) does not contribute to the intensity. If the detector can’t distinguish individual emitters, only photons emitted by the s-channel are detected. The radiation from the a-channel vanishes due to interference.

Now, evaluate the correlations

\[ \langle \eta^{+}_{1}\eta^{-}_{1}\rangle = \rho^{ss}_{ss} + \rho^{ss}_{ee}, \qquad \langle \eta^{+}_{2}\eta^{-}_{2}\rangle = \rho^{ss}_{aa} + \rho^{ss}_{ee} \]

We find the total intensity

\[ I(\mathbf{R}) = \Gamma(\mathbf{R})\left[2 (\rho^{ss}_{ee}+\rho^{ss}_{ss}) + \frac{\phi^2}{2}\left(\rho^{ss}_{aa}-\rho^{ss}_{ss}\right)\right] \]

When \(\Gamma_{12} > 0\) (the system is ergodic), \(\rho^{ss}_{ss}=\rho^{ss}_{aa}\) and thus \(I(\mathbf{R}) = 2 \Gamma(\mathbf{R}) (\rho^{ss}_{ee}+\rho^{ss}_{ss})\) regardless of \(\phi\). Integrating over the direction of \(\mathbf{R}\), the net intensity emitted by the dimer is \(I = 2 \gamma_{0} (\rho^{ss}_{ee}+\rho^{ss}_{ss})\) which is identical to the case where \(r_{12} > \lambda_{0}\). In terms of the radiation intensity, there is no superradiance for a dimer.

13.1.4. Second-order coherence#

\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} G^{(2)}(\tau)/\Gamma^2(\mathbf{R}) &= (4-2 \phi^2) \langle \eta^{+}_{1}(0)\eta^{+}_{1}(\tau) \eta^{-}_{1}(\tau) \eta^{-}_{1}(0)\rangle \\ &+\phi^2 \left[\langle\eta^{+}_{1}(0)\eta^{+}_{2}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{2}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{1}(0)\rangle + \langle\eta^{+}_{2}(0)\eta^{+}_{1}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{1}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{2}(0)\rangle\right] \\ &-\phi^2 \left[\langle\eta^{+}_{1}(0)\eta^{+}_{1}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{2}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{2}(0)\rangle - \langle\eta^{+}_{1}(0)\eta^{+}_{2}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{1} (\tau)\eta^{-}_{2}(0)\rangle \right]\\ &+ \phi^2 \left[\langle\eta^{+}_{2}(0)\eta^{+}_{1}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{2}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{1}(0)\rangle - \langle\eta^{+}_{1}(0)\eta^{+}_{2}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{2}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{1}(0)\rangle\right] \\ &+2 i \phi \left[ \langle\eta^{+}_{1}(0)\eta^{+}_{1}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{1}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{2}(0)\rangle - \langle\eta^{+}_{2}(0)\eta^{+}_{1}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{1}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{1}(0)\rangle\right] \\ &+2 i \phi \left[ \langle\eta^{+}_{1}(0)\eta^{+}_{1}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{2}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{1}(0)\rangle - \langle\eta^{+}_{1}(0)\eta^{+}_{2}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{1}(\tau)\eta^{-}_{1}(0)\rangle\right] \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

and the normalized second-order coherence function

\[ g^{(2)}(\tau) = \frac{G^{(2)}(\tau)}{4\left(\rho^{ss}_{ee}+\rho^{ss}_{ss}\right)^2} \]

The no-delay function is

(13.3)#\[ g^{(2)}(0) = \frac{(4-\phi^2)\,\rho^{ss}_{ee}}{4\left(\rho^{ss}_{ee}+\rho^{ss}_{ss}\right)^2} \]

For \(r_{12} > 0\), we can substitute the steady state (13.1) into Eq. (13.3). The result is simply

\[ g^{(2)}(0) = 1 - \frac{\phi^2}{4} \]

which approaches 1 as \(\phi \rightarrow 0\).

For \(r_{12}=0\) and thus \(\phi=0\), we use the steady state (13.2) and obtain

\[ g^{(2)}(0) = 1 - \frac{q}{(1+q)^2} \]

with \(q = e^{-\omega_{0}/T}\), which is bounded between \(\frac{3}{4}\) and \(1\).
