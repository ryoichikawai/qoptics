Trimer: Theoretical model

16. Trimer: Theoretical model#

This page is still under construction.

We consider a case where the distance between every pair of emitters is identical, that is \(r_{12} = r_{23} = r_{31}\). Hence, geometrically the structure belongs to \(D_{3h}\) symmetry group. However, the symmetry of the whole system including the radiation field can be quite different depending on the orientation of the dipole moment. We also need to recall that the orientation of the dipole moment is identical to all emitters.

When the dipole moment is perpendicular to the plane of the triangle (See Fig. 16.1), the space inversion with respect to \(z=0\) reverse the direction of the dipole moment. Hence its symmetry is \(D_{3}\) which is isomorphic to \(S_3\).

If the dipole moment is in-plane, it is not possible to have a high symmetry such as \(S_{3}\), \(D_{3}\) nor \(C_{3}\). When the moment is parallel to a symmetric axis (the left triangle in Fig. 16.2), mirror symmetry with respect to \(y\) axis is preserved. Hence, it has \(C_{2}\) symmetry. Otherwise, there is no symmetry and it belong to \(C_{1}\) group. Hence, it is similar to a scalene triangle.
